Standups

The morning check-in, without the meeting

Each person gets a direct message at a sensible local hour. The answers land as one formatted summary in your channel, so nobody sits through a call to hear what they could have read in a minute.

  • Your own questions, not a fixed template
  • Per-person timezones, so nobody is asked at midnight
  • Blockers highlighted, people on leave skipped rather than nagged
  • A private nudge for whoever has not filed by report time

Every standup carries fourteen days of completion, so a schedule that is slipping says so before anyone goes looking.

Coffee chats

Introduce the people who never talk

Pick a channel and a cadence. Everyone in it gets paired and introduced in a group message, and the pair settles on an hour that works for both of them rather than trading messages about it.

  • Never repeats a pairing until everyone has met
  • Groups of two to eight; an odd one out joins a three rather than sitting out
  • Hours that suit both timezones, picked with a button
  • Zoom books the meeting at the hour they agree
  • Asks afterwards whether they actually met

Settings on the left, the Slack message they produce on the right, updating as you type.

Kudos

Praise that means something because it runs out

Everyone gets a handful of tokens a day to give away, and unused ones do not carry over. Scarcity is the whole mechanic: unlimited praise is worth nothing, and a daily budget is what makes people actually spend it.

  • Pick your own token and daily amount
  • Resets at midnight in each person's own timezone
  • Leaderboards for giving, not only for receiving

Set the token and the allowance, and see the message your team will get.

Who runs what

One person per feature, not one admin for everything

Most tools give you admin or nothing, so the person who should own the standups ends up holding the API keys as well. Here a grant is per feature.

  • The team lead runs the standups
  • Someone in HR runs coffee chats and kudos
  • Neither can mint an API key or publish your standups at a public URL
  • The installer always counts as an admin, so a workspace cannot lock itself out

Press a chip to hand a feature over. Press it again to take it back.

Getting started

Three steps, and the first one is the long one

1

Put it somewhere

Helm on a cluster you already have, or Docker Compose on a small VPS or a spare Mac. It needs Postgres and an HTTPS URL Slack can reach; a Cloudflare tunnel is enough.

2

Add it to Slack

Create the app from the manifest in the repo, install it, and whoever installs it becomes the first admin.

3

Point it at a channel

Set the questions, the hour and who takes part. Turn on coffee chats and kudos when you want them; they are off until you do.

Self-hosting

Your database, your cluster, your rules

Postgres you control

Every standup, pairing and kudo lives in a database you own. No telemetry leaves the box.

Helm or Compose

A published chart at charts.morgenruf.dev, or Docker Compose on a spare machine. Migrations run themselves on start.

No open ports needed

Runs behind a Cloudflare tunnel, so a machine with no ingress controller and no public IP is fine.

Ask it questions

An MCP server with seventeen tools, so Claude, Cursor or Copilot can query your standup history directly.

Signed webhooks

HMAC-signed callbacks on every completed standup, for whatever you build next.

Switch parts off

Each feature is a module. Turn off what you do not use and it stops running and leaves the sidebar.

Questions

The ones people actually ask

Is it really free?

Yes, for any number of people. It is MIT licensed and there is no hosted tier to upsell you. You run it, so you pay for the server and the database and nothing else.

Can I run it without Kubernetes?

Yes. Docker Compose is a copy of the env file and one command, and it runs happily on a small VPS or a spare Mac mini. The Helm chart is for people who already have a cluster, not the only route.

Where does my standup data live?

In your own Postgres, and nowhere else. The only outbound calls are to Slack, to whichever AI provider you configure for summaries (optional, off by default), and to Resend if you switch on digest email.

Is this a Geekbot, Donut or HeyTaco alternative?

It covers what teams use those for: async standups, random coffee chats, and peer recognition. Running one app instead of three is most of the point, along with owning the data. It is not a clone of any of them, and if something you rely on is missing, open an issue and say so.

Does it work with Microsoft Teams or Google Chat?

Google Chat is in beta: standups collected and posted, with the same commands. Teams as a platform is in progress. A Teams meeting link can already be used as a coffee chat room, which is a different thing.

Can somebody run standups without being a workspace admin?

Yes. Grants are per feature, so the team lead can own the standups while somebody else owns coffee chats, and neither of them can mint an API key or publish your standups publicly.

Will it cope with a large team?

Delivery is per person and resumable, so a big channel is a queue rather than a burst, and a restart mid-round picks up where it stopped instead of messaging everybody twice.
